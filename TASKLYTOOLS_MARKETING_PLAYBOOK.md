# TasklyTools Marketing Playbook

- Prepared: 2026-11-05
- Scope: Phase 6 organic content and distribution plan
- Status: planning document only; no accounts, posts, messages, or ads were created

## 1. Positioning

**TasklyTools helps freelancers and small service businesses finish the business side of their work: invoices, pricing, proposals, profit, financial tracking, and campaign links.**

The free tools complete a single task. The guides explain the decision behind it. The paid
toolkits provide editable documents, spreadsheets, and a repeatable workflow for people who
want the whole process in one place.

### What TasklyTools is not

- Not accounting, tax, or legal advice
- Not an all-in-one CRM or enterprise finance platform
- Not a promise of income, clients, savings, or business growth
- Not a source of fake reviews, scarcity, or inflated results

## 2. Audience and problems

### Primary: solo freelancers

Typical work: design, development, writing, marketing, consulting, creative services.

Problems:

- Invoicing is inconsistent or missing important fields
- Rates are based on competitor guesses rather than costs and available hours
- Proposals are long on credentials and short on scope, price, and a decision step
- Client admin lives across email, notes, and disconnected files
- Project prices are set before the work is understood

Best entry points:

- Freelance rate calculator
- Invoice generator
- Proposal generator
- Freelancer Business Toolkit
- Proposal & Client Acquisition Toolkit

### Secondary: small service business owners

Typical work: studios, agencies, consultancies, local service businesses, small teams.

Problems:

- Revenue is visible, but profit is not
- Expenses are tracked inconsistently
- Pricing decisions ignore delivery cost and overhead
- Month-end review has no repeatable routine
- Invoices, receipts, and payment records are not connected

Best entry points:

- Profit calculator
- Profit guide
- Financial tracking guide
- Small Business Profit & Finance Toolkit

### Tertiary: solo marketers and founders

Typical work: campaign planning, content, paid social, email, partnerships.

Problems:

- Campaign links use inconsistent names
- Reports cannot separate source, medium, and campaign
- Teams rename parameters after launch
- UTM links are created but not checked before publishing

Best entry points:

- UTM builder
- UTM guide
- Free project planning checklist

## 4. Distribution rules

- Use one clear reader problem per post.
- Lead with a useful explanation, checklist, or example; link second.
- Match the CTA to the reader's stage: tool for a task, guide for a decision, toolkit for a workflow.
- Add UTM parameters to every external campaign link.
- Do not automate direct messages, mass-tag people, or post the same text across communities.
- Do not use fake testimonials, invented customer counts, or claims that a tool guarantees results.
- Do not present financial calculators as accounting or tax advice.
- Keep the free checklist free and available without email.
- Do not promote a paid toolkit as purchasable until checkout is live. Product pages can still be
  linked as previews while checkout is disconnected.

## 5. UTM conventions

Use lowercase, hyphens, and one naming system.

| Parameter | Convention | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `utm_source` | Platform or place | `linkedin`, `youtube`, `newsletter`, `community` |
| `utm_medium` | Distribution type | `social`, `video`, `email`, `referral` |
| `utm_campaign` | Campaign or series | `freelance-admin`, `pricing-foundations` |
| `utm_content` | Specific asset or variation | `carousel-invoice-fields`, `short-rate-formula` |
| `utm_term` | Optional audience or keyword | `freelancers`, `small-business` |

Example:

`https://tasklytools.org/guides/how-to-create-a-professional-freelancer-invoice/?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=freelance-admin&utm_content=carousel-invoice-fields`

Rules:

- Never use spaces or uppercase letters.
- Never put personal information in a UTM value.
- Keep one campaign name across every asset in the same series.
- Record the final URL in the content calendar before publishing.
- Check the link in an incognito window before publishing.

### Post-writing template

1. **Hook:** name the specific problem.
2. **Context:** one sentence on why it matters.
3. **Useful body:** three to five concrete points.

6. **Boundary:** add "not accounting or tax advice" when the post discusses profit or pricing.

## 9. Five community approaches

### 1. Answer an invoicing question

- Where: freelance, design, development, or small-business communities that allow resource sharing.
- Contribution: explain the fields and terms that prevent confusion.
- Boundary: answer the question first; link only if the community allows it.
- CTA: invoice guide.

### 2. Share a pricing teardown

- Where: freelancer or studio communities with pricing discussions.
- Contribution: show how a project price is built from scope, time, risk, and margin.
- Boundary: do not ask for the reader's rates or private financial data.
- CTA: project pricing guide or rate calculator.

### 3. Offer a proposal outline

- Where: freelancer and consultant communities.
- Contribution: post a short proposal structure with a clear decision step.
- Boundary: use a generic example, not a real client.
- CTA: proposal guide.

### 4. Explain a finance routine

- Where: small-business owner communities.
- Contribution: share a weekly and monthly record-keeping routine.
- Boundary: do not give tax, legal, or accounting advice.
- CTA: financial tracking guide.

### 5. Run a UTM naming audit

- Where: marketing and analytics communities.
- Contribution: show a consistent naming convention and a pre-launch checklist.
- Boundary: do not ask people to post live campaign URLs with sensitive values.
- CTA: UTM guide or builder.

### Community rules

- Read the rules before posting.
- Disclose that the link is to your own site when relevant.
- Do not create multiple accounts or ask others to upvote.
- Do not copy the same comment across threads.
- If a community does not allow links, participate without one.

## 10. Five outreach templates

Use these only for relevant, individual conversations. Personalize the first line. Do not send
bulk messages or more than one follow-up.

### Template 1: Freelancer invoicing

> Hi [Name], I saw your post about [specific invoicing question]. The usual cause is usually
> a missing field or an unclear payment term, not a difficult client. I wrote a short checklist
> covering the fields, dates, and review step: [UTM link]. No reply needed if it is not useful.

### Template 2: Freelance pricing

> Hi [Name], your question about [specific pricing issue] is a common one. The useful starting
> point is the cost base and the number of hours you can actually bill. There is a worked
> example here: [UTM link]. If you share the project type, I can point you to the relevant
> section.

### Template 3: Proposal review

> Hi [Name], I read your note about [specific proposal problem]. A proposal usually gets easier
> to approve when it separates the client's problem, the included scope, the price, and the next
> step. I put a simple outline here: [UTM link]. Happy to leave it there if you already have a
> process.

### Template 4: Small-business finance

> Hi [Name], I saw your question about [specific profit or tracking issue]. A monthly review is
> easier when revenue, expenses, and profit use the same categories each time. There is a plain-
> language walkthrough here: [UTM link]. This is general information, not accounting or tax
> advice.

### Template 5: UTM campaign links

> Hi [Name], your campaign reporting question sounds like a naming-consistency problem. A short
> convention for source, medium, campaign, and content usually makes the report much easier to
> read. Here is the checklist I use: [UTM link]. No follow-up needed if it is not relevant.

### Outreach boundaries

- No scraped lists.
- No mass identical messages.
- No pretending to be a customer or researcher.
- No pressure, false deadlines, or invented availability.
- One follow-up maximum, after a reasonable delay.
- Honor "no thanks" immediately.

## 12. Weekly workflow

1. **Monday:** choose one pillar and one reader problem.
2. **Tuesday:** draft the post and the matching guide CTA.
3. **Wednesday:** create the UTM link and check it in an incognito window.
4. **Thursday:** publish the primary asset and reply to substantive comments.
5. **Friday:** reuse the idea in one other format, then record the result.
6. **Monthly:** review which guide and tool links earned clicks; revise the weakest hooks.

Do not judge success by impressions alone. Track qualified clicks to guides and tool pages,
resource downloads, and product-page visits. Do not claim revenue attribution while checkout is
disconnected.

## 13. Measurement

Use the analytics plan in `TASKLYTOOLS_ANALYTICS_PLAN.md`.

Minimum monthly review:

- Guide views by guide and cluster
- Tool starts and completed results
- Copy, print, and export actions
- Resource download clicks
- Product page views
- Product CTA clicks by product and placement
- Referrals by source, medium, and campaign

Do not send names, email addresses, invoice contents, financial amounts, or other personal data
to analytics.
