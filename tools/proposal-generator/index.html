          How to use it

          Build a proposal in five steps

          Give the client enough detail to make a decision without turning the proposal into a contract.

          1. Name the project, your business, the client, and the dates.
          2. Describe the problem and the outcome in the overview, then set clear scope boundaries.
          3. List deliverables and timeline items one per line so the preview stays easy to scan.
          4. Add the investment amount, payment terms, and the next actions the client needs to take.
          5. Generate the preview, edit the wording directly, then copy the text or print it as a PDF.

          A useful proposal is specific

          • OverviewThe client's problem
          • ScopeWhat is and is not included
          • DeliverablesWhat the client receives
          • TimelineWhen each phase happens
          • InvestmentPrice and payment terms

          FAQ

          Proposal Generator questions

          Short answers about editing, copying, privacy, and contracts.

          How does the Proposal Generator work?

          Fill in the project, client, scope, deliverables, timeline, and investment fields, then generate the preview. The tool arranges your answers into a structured proposal in your browser.

          Is the Proposal Generator free?

          Yes. The TasklyTools Proposal Generator is free to use and does not require an account.

          Can I edit the generated proposal?

          Yes. Click any text section in the preview to edit the wording directly. Your edits stay in the preview until you generate again from the form.

          Can I copy or print the proposal?

          Yes. Copy text puts the current proposal on your clipboard as plain text. Print / Save PDF opens your browser print dialog and prints only the proposal page.

          Is my proposal data stored?

          No. The proposal is built locally in your browser. Your client details, scope, and pricing are not sent to a TasklyTools server.

          Does this create a legally binding contract?

          No. This tool helps format a proposal; it is not a contract and does not provide legal advice. Have important terms reviewed by a qualified professional.
